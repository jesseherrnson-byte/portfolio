Jesse was fascinated by computers from a very young age. At age six, he created a simple "make it to the end" game that was certainly legally distinct from Snakes and Ladders. It was Jesse's first experience in trying to “find the fun.”

Now, Jesse is a software engineer at PwC in Boston, MA. There, he works as a backend developer creating AI products used by thousands of employees across the firm. Jesse's background in education allow him to easily translate technical concepts to non-technical clients.

For college, Jesse attended Wesleyan University; while there, he took an intensive course on the game design process taught by Professor Christopher Weaver, founder of Bethesda Softworks. This course challenged teams of coders, designers, and artists, to create an educational children's video game, which would be playtested at a local elementary school. His team's game, One Earth, won the competition. In following semesters, Jesse became a course assistant to support future students in the creation of their games and to iteratively refine the course curriculum. Jesse graduated from Wesleyan in May of 2024, with a double major in Computer Science and Music, a minor in Film, and a focus on game design. In his final semesters, he supported the development of the Game Design minor at Wesleyan.

Jesse owes much of his interest in computer science to his time at URJ 6 Points Sci-Tech Academy, a science and technology camp where he first learned how to code (Processing/Java) and the basics of how to create games. He took the Video Game Design workshop five times as a camper and returned for many years as a staff and leadership member, strengthening his organizational and educational skills.
